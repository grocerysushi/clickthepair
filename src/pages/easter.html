How the Easter Board Works

Every tile on this board is part of Easter and spring. The set has two bunnies, a hatching chick and a baby chick, an egg, a basket, chocolate, candy, a lollipop and a carrot, along with tulips, cherry blossoms, yellow blossoms and a seedling. A butterfly, a bee, a ladybug, a lamb, a ribbon and a rainbow round it out. Each board shows 12 tiles, and exactly two of them match. Click both to score, and a new board appears.

Spring pastels and look-alike animals make the matching pair hard to spot from the first board on. You have 60 seconds, a wrong click costs 2 seconds, and your best Easter score is saved separately from the other modes.

A Quick Game for Easter Weekend

A round lasts one minute, so it fits in between the egg hunt and dinner. Take turns on one phone and see who finds the most pairs, or put it on the TV and play together.

After a round, the Share button sends your score with a link that family and friends can open to try to beat it. The game runs in any browser, with nothing to download and no account to make.

Tips for the Easter Board

  • Two bunnies, two chicks. One bunny is just a face and the other is the whole rabbit. One chick is still in its shell and the other faces you. Neither is a pair.
  • Sort the flowers. Tulips are cups, cherry blossoms are pink and flat, and the yellow blossom has a round center.
  • Check the sweets. Candy, the lollipop and chocolate often turn up together. Look at the wrapper and the stick.

Easter Board FAQ

Which emojis are on the Easter board?

There are 20 in the set: hatching chick, baby chick, rabbit face, rabbit, egg, tulip, cherry blossom, blossom, basket, chocolate, candy, carrot, butterfly, lamb, seedling, bee, ribbon, rainbow, ladybug and lollipop. Each board uses 11 of them, with one appearing twice.

Is this Easter game free?

Yes. It is free to play in any web browser on a phone, tablet or computer, with no download and no sign-up.

Is it harder than the classic game?

Usually, yes. With two bunnies, two chicks and lots of pastel flowers, it takes a closer look to find the pair than on the mixed classic board.
