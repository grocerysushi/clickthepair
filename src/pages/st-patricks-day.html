How the St. Patrick's Day Board Works

Every tile on this board is part of St. Patrick's Day. The set has a four-leaf clover, a rainbow, a bag of gold, a top hat, a boot and a fairy, along with a green heart, a frog, a snake, a castle and a sheep. Potatoes, bread, leafy greens, broccoli, cucumber and a green apple fill out the table, and a fiddle, a drum and a party popper bring the music. Each board shows 12 tiles, and exactly two of them match. Click both to score, and a new board appears.

This is the greenest board on the site, so the matching pair hides among shades of green from the first board on. You have 60 seconds, a wrong click costs 2 seconds, and your best St. Patrick's Day score is saved separately from the other modes.

A Quick Game for March 17

A round lasts one minute, which makes it an easy St. Patrick's Day party game. Put it on a big screen and race to call out the matches, or see who can find the most pairs and claim the pot of gold.

After a round, the Share button sends your score with a link that friends can open to try to beat it. The game runs in any browser, with nothing to download and no account to make.

Tips for the St. Patrick's Day Board

  • Not everything green is a clover. The clover, leafy greens, broccoli, cucumber and green apple all share a color. Look at the shape first.
  • Find the odd colors. The rainbow, the gold, the castle and the drum stand out against all the green, so they are quick to rule in or out.
  • Frog and snake. Both are green animals, but the frog is a round face and the snake is long and coiled.

St. Patrick's Day Board FAQ

Which emojis are on the St. Patrick's Day board?

There are 20 in the set: four-leaf clover, rainbow, money bag, top hat, potato, snake, fiddle, bread, leafy greens, broccoli, cucumber, green apple, green heart, frog, castle, sheep, fairy, party popper, drum and boot. Each board uses 11 of them, with one appearing twice.

Is this St. Patrick's Day game free?

Yes. It is free to play in any web browser on a phone, tablet or computer, with no download and no sign-up.

Why is there a snake on the board?

Legend says St. Patrick drove the snakes out of Ireland, so the snake has long been part of the day's stories and art.
